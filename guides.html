About This Library

What the Tank Calculator Guides Cover

Most tank content online falls into two buckets: supplier pages that want to sell you a vessel, or forum answers that assume you already know what a “2:1 elliptical head” is. This library is the third thing — a reference written to stand on its own, from the first tape measurement to the last strap on the truck. It exists because the calculator answers how much instantly, but why deserves slower reading: why a quarter-full horizontal cylinder holds less than a quarter of its volume, why dished ends add gallons a flat-ended formula ignores, why the liquid — not the tank — decides the weight, and why a tilted installation reads differently at each end.

The organization is deliberate. The six categories below follow the life of a job: measure the vessel, choose the liquid, run the math, walk the project through, set and fill it safely, and — when a quote or a nameplate uses a word you need to trust — check the reference. Within each category the topics are ordered roughly the way you will meet them. Cross-references point to the exact calculator input or companion tool each idea changes, so reading and doing stay attached instead of drifting apart — and the liquid weight table on the home page backs every density claim with the number behind it.

The library also scales with the reader. A homeowner sizing one rain barrel needs three stops; an operator staging a delivery needs three different ones. The reading-paths section that follows this intro sequences the same topics by audience — so you can either browse by category, as below, or jump to your role and follow a three-step trail. Both routes end at the calculator, because every guide here exists to change a number you can enter.

Two promises apply to every guide. First, no product pitches: the site sells nothing and is affiliated with no tank maker or fuel distributor, so recommendations end where calibration charts begin. Second, checkability: where a number appears, the arithmetic that produced it is either shown or one click away. A guide you cannot verify is just a longer advertisement.

The πr²L Rule: Tank Capacity in Your Head

Questions About the Guide Library

Are the guides written for professionals or DIYers?▼

Both, deliberately. Each topic opens with the practical rule a homeowner needs, then explains the reasoning a professional wants — why the rule exists, when it breaks, and what to check before trusting it. No prior tank experience is assumed anywhere in the library.

Do the guides replace the calculator?▼

No — they feed it. Reading explains what a head type or a specific gravity means; the calculator turns that understanding into numbers. Every guide links back to the input or tool where the idea becomes arithmetic.

How do I request a topic that is not covered?▼

Send it through the contact page: the question you were trying to answer, the job you were on, and what you wished had been written down. Requests describing a real field problem go to the top of the content queue — the library grows from real questions.

Are the worked example numbers real?▼

The examples use the same formulas the calculator runs and the same liquid gravities published in its own table, so every figure is reproducible with the stated inputs. They are illustrative rather than vessel-specific — final handling and placement decisions should always respect the governing code and the vessel's calibration chart.

Can I share or link to a guide?▼

Yes. Linking is the best way to share: it keeps the version current and the formulas visible. Quoting a short passage with attribution and a link is welcome; systematic republication is not — see the Terms of Service for the details.

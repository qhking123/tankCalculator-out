Liquid Density Comparison — SG, lb/gal, kg/L Side by Side

The short path from volume to weight runs through one number: specific gravity. Specific gravity (SG) is the ratio of a liquid's density to the density of pure water at its densest point (4 degrees Celsius, where water hits exactly 1.000 g/cm3). Since one US gallon of water weighs 8.34 pounds and one liter of water weighs exactly one kilogram, SG is numerically equal to lb/gal divided by 8.34, and equal to kg/L directly. That single ratio is why the formulaweight = gal x 8.34 x SG works for every liquid you can pour into a tank.

The Three Core Formulas That Power Every Conversion on This Page

Every number that comes out of this calculator traces back to three algebraic rearrangements of the same physical relationship: weight equals density times volume. Because specific gravity is dimensionless, the formulas work across unit systems — gallons and pounds, liters and kilograms, cubic feet and short tons. You do not need to memorize all three; if you remember the first one, you can derive the other two by simple algebra.

Volume to Weight (US)

lb = gal x 8.34 x SG

Gallons times the weight of one gallon of water (8.34 lb) times specific gravity. 50 gal of water = 50 x 8.34 x 1.0 = 417 lb.

Volume to Weight (Metric)

kg = liters x SG

One liter of water is exactly one kilogram by definition, so liters x SG = kg directly. No conversion factor needed.

Weight to Volume

gal = lb / (8.34 x SG)

Algebraic inverse. 30 lb of water = 30 / (8.34 x 1.0) = 3.6 gal. Same structure works for kg to liters: liters = kg / SG.

Quick Derivation — Why 8.34 Exactly?

One US liquid gallon is defined as 231 cubic inches. Water at its densest point (4 C) has a mass of 0.036063 pounds per cubic inch. Multiply: 231 x 0.036063 = 8.3395 pounds per gallon, which rounds to the commonly used 8.34. This factor is exact for water at 4 C and is the anchor that all the weight conversions on this site hang from. For quick mental math, 8.34 is close enough to 8 and 1/3 — so divide gallons by 3 and add one more gallon for every 3 you get: 15 gal water = 15 / 3 = 5, plus 5 = 10, plus a bit = 125 lb. Close enough for a sanity check on a quick weigh-in.

Why Winter Water Is Heavier Than Summer Water — Temperature Density Table

Almost every liquid expands when it warms up and contracts when it cools down, which means a fixed volume of hot liquid weighs less than the same volume cold. Water is a little unusual because it has a density maximum at 4 C — it is densest just above freezing, not at freezing. Above 4 C water expands steadily: a gallon of boiling water at 100 C weighs about 4.2% less than a gallon of 4 C water. For fuel oils the effect is worse: No. 2 diesel, which is stored and dispensed at whatever the tank temperature happens to be, can swing plus or minus 3% across a normal year of seasons. That 3% sounds small, but on a 10,000-gallon delivery it is 300 gallons and over 2,100 pounds of difference in load weight.

Three Mistakes That Will Break a Liquid Weight Calculation

1. Using Water Density for Every Liquid

The most common error. "A gallon weighs 8.34 pounds" is only true for water at 4 C. A gallon of gasoline weighs 6.17 pounds — the water overstatement is 36%. For a 100-gallon tank that is 217 pounds of phantom weight, enough to miss a truck weight-limit checkpoint by an axle group. The solution is simple: always ask what liquid is in the tank and multiply by its SG. The calculator on this page forces you through that question by giving you presets or a custom SG field.

2. Confusing US Gallons and Imperial Gallons

An Imperial gallon (UK, Canada pre-1970) is 4.54609 liters, 20% larger than the US liquid gallon's 3.78541. The weight of one Imperial gallon of water is 10.022 pounds, not 8.34. If you pull a dip chart from a UK supplier and treat every gallon as US, you will understate weight by about 17%. This calculator uses US gallons throughout because the fuel and tank industries in North America, Latin America, and most of Asia now standardize there — but the liter mode will catch both UK and EU users without ambiguity.

3. Ignoring Temperature When Fueling Vehicles

A semi-truck driver filling 120 gallons of diesel in January at 2 C (36 F) gets 120 gallons at SG 0.8400 — 1,008 pounds. The same fill in July at 35 C (95 F) is at SG roughly 0.8150 — 978 pounds. Same pump number, same invoice gallons, 30 pounds less actual fuel on the truck. The EPA's summer-blend gasoline rules compound this — summer gasoline is reformulated for vapor pressure, which typically drops SG another 0.01 compared to winter blend, for another 1.2% swing. Road weight enforcement in jurisdictions like California explicitly measures truck weight, not gallons, for exactly this reason.

Where Volume-to-Weight Actually Matters — Three Real-World Industries

Trucking and Rail Freight

Road weight limits are enforced in pounds or tonnes, not gallons. A typical Class 8 semi with two 120-gallon fuel tanks carries about 1,680 pounds of diesel full. That fuel weight counts against the truck's gross vehicle weight rating, so a driver deciding between a 3,500-lb pallet load or a 3,800-lb pallet load runs this exact calculation — and the difference between using diesel SG 0.84 (correct) and water SG 1.0 (wrong) on 240 gallons is 403 pounds of phantom weight, which can decide whether the truck clears a weigh station or gets flagged for reweigh.

Marine and Offshore Operations

Ship stability is calculated in tonnes of displacement, not cubic meters of hold volume. When a bulk carrier loads a mixed cargo — ore concentrates (SG 2.5-4.0), steel scrap (SG 7.85), and bags of cement (SG 1.44) — the loading master runs each liquid or bulk cargo through a density-to-weight chain to calculate the center of gravity. Get one SG wrong and you can either overload a frame member or, worse, mis-calculate roll stability. Our seawater preset (SG 1.025) is particularly important for buoyancy calculations — a boat that floats 6 inches deeper in salt water than in fresh water is a center-of-gravity problem waiting to happen.

Industrial Chemical Processing

Reaction stoichiometry runs on moles, which means grams and kilograms. When a 5,000-liter batch reactor is being charged with concentrated hydrochloric acid (SG 1.18), the volume on the dip chart does not tell the chemist how many moles of HCl are present — they need to convert liters to kg through SG, then kg to moles through molar mass. A 2% SG error (confusing 1.18 with 1.16) on a 5,000 liter charge is 116 kg of missing or excess acid, which can either waste product or produce an unsafe over-pressure event in the reactor.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert gallons to pounds for any liquid?▼

Multiply gallons by 8.34 (the weight of one US gallon of water in pounds) and then by the specific gravity (SG) of your liquid. The formula is: weight in pounds = gallons x 8.34 x SG. For water SG = 1.0, so 50 gallons of water = 50 x 8.34 x 1.0 = 417 pounds. For diesel fuel with SG 0.84, the same 50 gallons weighs 50 x 8.34 x 0.84 = 350 pounds. The liquid weight calculator on this page handles both US gallons and liters, bidirectional conversion, and lets you pick from seven common liquids or enter any custom SG.

Why is a gallon of diesel heavier than a gallon of gasoline?▼

Specific gravity. Diesel typically has SG around 0.84 while gasoline is 0.72 to 0.76 depending on the blend and season. That means a gallon of diesel weighs about 7.0 pounds while the same gallon of gasoline weighs about 6.2 pounds — roughly an 11% difference. This matters on road weight limits, which are often calculated on volume of diesel-equivalent, and on aircraft where Jet A-1 fuel load is a critical weight variable.

Does temperature change liquid weight?▼

Yes, temperature changes density and therefore weight per unit volume. Water is densest at 4 degrees Celsius (SG 1.000) and expands as it warms — at 100 C boiling point water has SG 0.958, so a gallon of boiling water weighs 4.2% less than a gallon of 4 C water. Fuel oils are worse: No. 2 diesel can vary by plus or minus 3% across normal summer-winter temperature swings. The calculator on this page includes a temperature-correction option that adjusts the density of water to the temperature you specify, and gives warnings about fuels where temperature matters more than a simple SG table can capture.

How heavy is 15 gallons of water?▼

15 gallons x 8.34 pounds per gallon = 125.1 pounds. That is a common tote or bucket size used by homeowners, painters, and boaters — and it is heavier than most people expect until they lift one. The same 15 gallons of diesel weighs 15 x 8.34 x 0.84 = 105 pounds, and 15 gallons of gasoline weighs only 92.7 pounds because gasoline is less dense.

How do I convert 30 pounds of water to gallons?▼

Divide the weight by (8.34 x SG). For water SG = 1.0, so gallons = 30 / 8.34 = 3.6 gallons. For diesel the same 30 pounds converts to 30 / (8.34 x 0.84) = 4.29 gallons. This is the inverse of the weight formula and is the mode B calculation on this page — useful when you know how much something weighs and want to back into what volume that represents in your tank.

What is the difference between specific gravity and density?▼

Specific gravity is the ratio of a liquid's density to the density of water at a reference temperature (usually 4 C). Since water's density is almost exactly 1 kilogram per liter (and 8.34 pounds per US gallon), SG is numerically equal to density in g/cm3 or kg/L — which makes it a convenient shorthand. A liquid with SG 1.2 is 20% denser than water; a liquid with SG 0.85 is 15% less dense. All the conversions on this page flow through SG so you can enter one number and get pounds, kilograms, and tons simultaneously.

Does this calculator work for metric tons?▼

Yes. One metric tonne is 1,000 kilograms. If you enter your volume in liters and pick any liquid, the calculator returns the kilogram weight and you can divide by 1,000 to get tonnes. Or use the weight-to-volume mode and enter a target tonnage directly in the pounds field (1 tonne = 2,204.62 pounds) and back out the volume. The results panel on this page shows pounds, kilograms, and tonnes automatically whenever you use volume-to-weight mode.

Can I use this for propane or other liquefied gases?▼

Propane has SG 0.58 when stored as a liquid under pressure, but liquid density is highly pressure and temperature dependent for LPG products — which is why we do not list it as a preset here. Use the custom SG field with propane's liquid density at your storage pressure and temperature, or head to the dedicated propane tank size calculator on this site which handles the pressure-density relationship properly for LPG.

Common Questions About These Terms

Four questions that cover most real situations. The formal sections above govern; these explain them.

Can I use calculator results in a client quote or estimate?▼

Yes — that is exactly what planning use means. Run your own inputs, cite the result as your own planning figure, and verify it against the calibration chart for the tank you specify. What you may not do is republish the calculator itself or present the output as certified data.

Can I link to the site from my blog, course, or forum post?▼

Please do. Links are the cleanest way to share the tool: they keep the calculations on the site, keep the formulas visible, and keep the version current. Quoting a short result with attribution and a link is welcomed.

The calculator disagrees with my calibration chart — who wins?▼

The chart. The calculator models your tank with idealized geometry and the nominal density you select; the calibration chart accounts for things the model cannot see, like as-built dimensions, internal fittings, and shell deformation. Use the difference to sharpen your inputs, not to overwrite the chart.

Do these terms change if I use the site professionally?▼

The terms are the same for everyone — there is no professional tier and no special license. The practical difference is accountability: on a commercial job, the contractor of record remains responsible for tank selection, foundation design, and code compliance, whatever a planning tool suggested.
